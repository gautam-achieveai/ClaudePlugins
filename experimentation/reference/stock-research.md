# Long-term stock theses

Use competing hypotheses and scenario valuation. Separate **business quality**, **attractiveness at today's price**, and **portfolio fit**. A good business can already have demanding expectations in its price.

- Define horizon, comparator/benchmark and downside constraints. For a methodology question, do not invent a ticker recommendation or demand portfolio details unnecessarily.
- State mechanisms and falsifiable operating predictions: durable pricing power versus temporary demand; productive reinvestment versus growth bought with poor returns. Check filings, accounting notes, cash flows, debt, dilution, customer concentration and competitor evidence as relevant.
- Ask what assumptions justify the price. A reverse valuation infers expectations under a model; it does not directly observe market beliefs. Model plausible adverse, central and favorable outcomes with sensitivity checks. Use deterministic calculations.
- Preserve original assumptions and dates. Revisit on material filings, forecast checkpoints and thesis-breaking evidence. A scenario is conditional arithmetic, not proof of its assumptions.
- Track business-prediction accuracy separately from investment returns, including distributions, benchmark and relevant costs. A profitable outcome alone does not validate its explanation.

Historical evaluation must use information available then, include failures/delistings, and reserve later cases. Date-filtered retrieval cannot remove future information embedded in model weights. Use retrospective cases for generating and challenging lessons; a prospective forecast record provides stronger evidence of transfer.

Use agent research memory to preserve the thesis, evidence and revisions. Do not claim Arbor, GEPA or this plugin is a proven market-beating method. Research authorization is not trade authorization. Use an available investing workflow for specialized valuation/data work when it genuinely helps; it is not a mandatory dependency.
