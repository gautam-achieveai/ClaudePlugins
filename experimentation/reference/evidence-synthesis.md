# Evidence before conclusions

For each material claim, keep its source locator, supporting observation, contrary evidence, applicability and remaining gap. An outline or claim table is useful when it exposes missing support; avoid creating one for a trivial answer.

## Appraise the source and the inference

- Open the underlying document or data. Record author/organization, publication or data period, version, retrieval date and exact section/table/page/query. If only an abstract or snippet is available, state that limit and narrow the claim.
- Check the method, population/task, comparator, measurement and uncertainty. Authority, recency, peer review and citation count are signals, not substitutes for this check.
- For LLM studies, compare tested models, tools, prompts, retrieval corpus, actual budget, baseline strength and evaluator. Separate retrieval quality, evidence utilization, synthesis quality and presentation. Do not apply an older small-model workaround automatically to a stronger model.
- Trace source lineage. Two reports, reposts or agents citing the same experiment are not independent confirmations. Keep secondary details that add information without counting another study.
- Separate observations, author interpretations, the current researcher's inference and simulation. Cite each decisive factual claim near its supporting source. A bundle of citations at a paragraph's end may not support every sentence.

## Resolve disagreement without erasing it

Compare definitions, populations, dates, budgets, interventions and outcome measures. Apparent conflict may be a difference in conditions. Preserve genuine disagreement when data cannot resolve it; do not average incompatible metrics or vote by number of sources. Ask which missing observation would change the next action.

An evidence reviewer verifies a specific claim or validity risk against raw material. Agreement among agents is not independent evidence. A readable synthesis must still be faithful to its sources. Report grounded conclusions, conditional inferences and unknowns distinctly; avoid invented numerical confidence.

## Turn findings into leads

A lead is an actionable unresolved question, promising mechanism or missing source—not an established fact. Give it an ID, why it could change the decision, evidence/assumption links, owner (or unassigned), next research action or test, and a stop/reopening condition. See [records](${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/reference/records.md) for persistence without a new schema.

Examples:
- **Compression:** a paper's gains at one budget suggest testing whether exact constraints plus summaries transfer to the target reader. The paper does not prove a generic winner.
- **Product:** usage correlation suggests competing explanations; missing non-adopter evidence becomes an interview/data lead, not an automatic feature decision.
- **Stocks:** reported margin expansion leads to a check of segment mix, temporary effects and price expectations. It does not itself establish investment attractiveness.

End with the answer, decisive evidence, strongest objection and next lead. Preserve the research brief in the same study if work will continue; one-off answers need no permanent store. The [shared loop](${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/reference/research-loop.md) carries these findings into experiments and brings results back to research.
